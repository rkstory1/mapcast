# Script rules

## Structure of a long animated explainer (10 to 20 minutes)
1. Hook (0:00 to 0:30): a story or fact, the open question, the promise. See research.md.
2. The map (10 s): the sections the viewer will get. The progress map (`tour` beat) shows them on screen.
3. Sections, each 1.5 to 3 minutes: the idea, a true story or real data, a twist, one thing to do. Each section opens
   with the progress map so viewers always know where they are (a re-hook every section).
4. A re-hook every 45 to 60 seconds: a new question, a surprising number, "but here is the catch".
5. The user's own part: a framework, card, checklist or worked example of their own. YouTube rewards a human
   contribution, and it is what makes the video theirs, not a summary anyone could make.
6. Close: back to the opening story, the one-line takeaway, a comment question ("Tell us in the comments..."),
   subscribe. A disclaimer part for money, health or legal topics ("This video is for educational purposes only and
   is not financial advice.").

## Writing for a voice
- One part = one fenced block of 10 to 30 seconds (about 30 to 80 words), with a `### NN Title` heading above it.
- Start each block with one direction tag: [curious], [warm], [calm], [serious], [excited].
- Short, clear, spoken sentences. Conversational, cinematic, smooth transitions between parts.
- Numbers as they are said: "twenty five", "nineteen eighty seven", "three point five million". Captions turn them
  back into digits (numbers.txt).
- No long dashes (TTS stumbles); prefer statements to questions (TTS reads questions flat). Avoid lists in brackets,
  abbreviations and symbols.
- Names the voice may mispronounce: write them the way they sound, or test one part first.
- Spoken pace is about 160 to 180 words a minute: 1,600 words is about 10 minutes.

## Facts
- Every number, quote and story checked at the primary source (the book, the paper, the official site), logged in
  brief.md with the source. Summaries and AI answers get details wrong; go to the source.
- Short quotes only (one line), credited on screen. Paraphrase everything else.
- If a claim cannot be confirmed, cut it or say clearly that it is disputed.

## YouTube's monetisation rules (2026)
- Mass-produced, near-identical videos are demonetised as "inauthentic content". Every video needs its own script,
  scenes and drawings, and a human pass on the script.
- No AI avatar or synthetic presenter, especially on money, health or legal topics. A voice over drawings is fine.
- No compilations of other people's clips or speeches. No copied scripts, art, titles or thumbnails.
- Disclose an AI voice with "Altered or synthetic content: Yes" at upload, and say so in the description.
