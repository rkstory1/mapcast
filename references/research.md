# Research: topic, titles, thumbnail, hook

The order the biggest YouTubers use: decide what makes someone click (title and thumbnail) and stay (the hook)
before writing a word of the script. A great script under a weak title is never watched.

## 1. Topic
- Proven demand first. Search the topic and its obvious phrasings with `./py ytsearch.py "q1" "q2" "q3"`. Look for
  videos with big views that are 1 to 5 years old: the demand is real and lasting. A topic where only big channels
  win is harder for a new channel; a topic where some small channels (under 10K subscribers) have 100K+ views is open.
- The copycat check. Search the exact angle you would use and sort by upload date in your head: if 5+ fresh
  uploads from faceless channels on that angle have 1 to 500 views, the angle is flooded. Change the angle, not the
  topic: tell it through one true story, one surprising number or one framework nobody else uses.
- Seasonality: habits and self-improvement peak in late December and January; money topics around tax season and
  New Year; back-to-school topics in August. Upload 2 to 4 weeks before the peak so the video is indexed.
- Long-form animated summaries win on watch time: 10 to 20 minutes of real value, each section useful on its own.

## 2. Titles (3, for YouTube's Test & compare)
- Curiosity first, keyword second: a concrete promise or a strange exact detail, then the search words after a bar.
  Patterns that work: "<Topic>: How <surprising story>", "<Topic> in N Minutes | The Complete Animated Summary",
  "The N <things> That <result> | <Topic> Explained".
- Search every candidate in quotes. If a fresh channel already uses it word for word, drop it.
- Under about 70 characters so it is not cut on phones. No clickbait the video does not pay off.

## 3. Thumbnail direction (one idea, three versions later)
- It complements the title, never repeats it: the title says what, the thumbnail shows the feeling or the number.
- One bold background colour from the palette, 2 to 3 huge words (at most 4), one chip (a number or a short phrase),
  one big drawing. No small text. Readable at 246 px wide (phone search results): check the small sheet.
- Contrast at least 4.5:1 for all text; dark ink on bright colours, white on ink.
- `THUMBS=out/research ./py ytsearch.py "topic"` saves the winning thumbnails as a sheet: study what the top ones do
  (colour, word count, face or object), then do something that stands out next to them.

## 4. The hook (first 30 seconds, written as spoken)
- By second 8: the story, the person or the surprising fact. No "welcome back", no "in this video" opening.
- By second 20: the open question the video answers, and a reason to stay to the end (a tool, a system, a payoff).
- Then a 10-second promise of what is coming (the sections), and straight in.
- Pay it off at the end: close back on the opening story.

## 5. Show the pitch
In brief.md: why this topic now (with the numbers), the copycat check, 3 titles, the thumbnail direction, the hook,
the section plan, the user's own contribution, the target length. Ask the user to pick and approve. Then the script.
